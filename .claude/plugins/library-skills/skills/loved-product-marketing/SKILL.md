# Loved — Martina Lauchengco

## Core frame

Product excellence is necessary but not sufficient; the market determines the value of the strategy, and someone must deliberately shape how the world discovers, understands, and adopts the product. That role is product marketing, whose purpose is to **drive product adoption by shaping market perception through strategic marketing activities that meet business goals**. The job is four fundamentals practiced together — Ambassador, Strategist, Storyteller, Evangelist — anchored by a PM/PMM partnership ("the go-to-market yin to product management's product yang") and made concrete through a small set of shared artifacts and cadences rather than campaign volume.

## Key concepts

- **Four fundamentals** — 1. *Ambassador*: connect customer and market insights into every team's decisions. 2. *Strategist*: direct the product's go-to-market. 3. *Storyteller*: shape how the world thinks about the product (positioning + messaging). 4. *Evangelist*: enable others — sales, analysts, influencers, customers, communities — to tell the story.
- **Positioning vs. messaging** — positioning is the place the product holds in customers' minds (the long game, years); messaging is what you say now to reinforce it (the short game, context-specific). Messaging aggregates into position over time.
- **Positioning = your actions + others'** — ~70% of buying decisions form beyond your control (the dark funnel: reviews, forums, comparison sites, word of mouth, search). Word-of-mouth becomes de facto positioning if you don't feed it.
- **Product Go-to-Market (PGTM) Canvas** — one-sheet living plan owned by PMM, four swim lanes: Customer/Outside Environment, Product Milestones, Marketing Strategies, Key Activities. Deliberately lightweight; built backward from year-end; revised quarterly cross-functionally.
- **One-sheet messaging canvas** — customer segments, positioning statement, key support messages (pillars), value areas per pillar, audience symbols, evidence/proof points, CTA, FAQs.
- **CAST** — messaging gut-check: Clear (curiosity, not comprehensiveness), Authentic (customer feels known), Simple (difference easy to grasp), Tested (iterated in the context customers experience it). Formulas ("X is the Y for Z that…") are input to thinking, never output.
- **Release scale** — calibrated levels (customer impact low/med/high → marketing objectives, resources, lead times) giving product and GTM a shared vocabulary; Level 1 = minimum work; target one to two top-level releases per year.
- **Agile marketing** — PMM-led weekly scrum of the cross-functional marketing squad: "learnings in," not "reporting out"; many small experiments over few big bets; data over opinion.
- **Six GTM models** — direct sales, inside sales, channel partners, direct to professional/customer, trial/freemium, product-led growth.
- **Evangelism ≠ promotion** — promotion is product-facing (features, "look at us"); evangelism is customer-outcome-facing (stories, day-in-the-life demos, what changed in the world) and is systematically enabled, not bought.
- **Adoption life cycle as GTM guide** — Innovators→Laggards typically 7–10+ years; each stage changes price, distribution, message (iPhone: $599 exclusive → $199+App Store → Verizon+FaceTime → emotional simplicity).
- **Pricing/packaging levers** — price is perceived value, not cost; monetization ≠ pricing ≠ packaging; editions map to segments/use cases; decision fatigue kills purchases.
- **Lighthouse customers & sales playbook** — reference customers prove it works; the playbook turns their pattern into repeatable stage-by-stage actions, tools, and qualification criteria.
- **Market sensing** — a standing practice, not a project: direct customer contact weekly, standard open-ended questions, insights written down and piped into product and GTM discussions. Insight ≠ requirement; keep only insights that change a decision.

## Heuristics

- **First marketing hire = product marketing**, director-level, before demand gen or comms; specialists amplify a foundation, they can't create one.
- **PMM:PM ratio** — 1:1 to 1:5, average ~1:2.5; embed the PMM in the product squad with a designated PM partner.
- **PM/PMM cadence:** weekly product-squad meeting (minimum); bimonthly/monthly product planning reviews; quarterly PGTM + roadmap review (sales cycles lag ≥ a quarter).
- **Marketing cadence:** weekly alignment/agile scrum; monthly activity + funnel review against intended outcomes; quarterly PGTM revision.
- **Sales cadence:** weekly/biweekly pipeline reviews (PMM attends); monthly joint funnel analysis with SLAs on handoffs; quarterly pipeline-vs-goal reckoning.
- **Fix the bottom of the funnel first** — with a salesforce involved, no conversion at the end makes more top-of-funnel pointless.
- **Beware paid budget masking a weak organic foundation** — content, comparisons, reviews, forums, testimonials must be healthy before ads scale anything.
- **Six strategy themes** to pick GTM strategies from: enable growth to a business goal; improve conversion of specific customers; generate awareness/discovery/brand; define or lead a category; engender validation/loyalty/evangelism; develop new segments/partners.
- **Category:** redefinition beats creation (an existing "you are here" anchor); "competition isn't a crowd, it's confirmation" — you need external evidence either way.
- **Discovery is timeboxed** — a week to a month of probing (win/loss, sales shadowing, exit surveys, A/B message tests, ad-direction tests, search-journey tests), then execute and iterate. Probe early, probe often; market fit must be *re*discovered as product and market move.
- **Early B2B pricing** — customized deals are fine until ~20–30 customers, then set repeatable guardrails. Premium test: no one complaining about price = money on the table.
- **Anti-patterns** (diagnose these): marketing as a service desk to sales; style over substance; stuck in technical weeds; chasing SEO clicks that fight positioning; shipping what sales can't sell (PMM absent from planning); release timing set by completion, not adoption.
- **Hiring screen** — "tell me about great marketing you admire, and now compete against it": evaluate breadth of toolset, adaptation to new constraints ("now with $250k"), openness to new information; curiosity/adaptability > polish.

## When to apply

- **"What marketing org/roles do we need?"** → start with one PMM per product team (four fundamentals), a small cross-functional specialist squad (content, digital/growth, comms/design) run as a weekly agile scrum, sales enablement only if there's a salesforce; ratios and cadences above.
- **"Plan the launch / GTM."** → build the PGTM canvas in a 3-hour cross-functional kickoff (hour 1: what's working/market realities/SWOT; hours 2–3: canvas); classify the release on the release scale; work back from the date for lead times.
- **"Write positioning/messaging."** → messaging discovery interviews → one-sheet canvas via the 7 steps → CAST check → test in real customer contexts (page, email, ad), not in a document.
- **"Small budget, need traffic/signups."** → organic foundation + dark funnel first (search journey, comparisons, reviews, communities, evangelism enablement); many small paid experiments through the agile scrum; judge by funnel stage conversion, bottom-up.
- **"How should marketing and product work together?"** → embedded PMM, the touchpoint cadences, PMM voice in release timing and roadmap; the "indicators it's working" lists.
- **"Where are we on adoption?"** → place the product on the life cycle; adjust price, channel, and message to the current adopter segment; pick the segment that fuels *future* growth, not just any customer.
