# Product Strategy Frameworks — the right lens at the right altitude

## Core frame

Strategy frameworks are not interchangeable. Each answers a different question at a different altitude. Twelve frameworks, sequenced for an enterprise B2B middleware/platform PM: start at the top (Playing to Win sets boundaries), move down through customer truth (JTBD), market frame (Obviously Awesome), business model (Platform), landscape (Wardley), then product planning (OST, RICE, Kano, North Star), then GTM (PLG/SLG, Land-and-Expand, Category). Pick by question, not by fashion — and apply the coherence test: every choice should reinforce the others.

## Key concepts

- **Playing to Win (Lafley & Martin):** five cascading choices — Winning Aspiration → Where to Play → How to Win → Capabilities → Management Systems. Strategy is integrated choice, not vision. Coherence test: each choice reinforces the others; contradictions mean the strategy is incoherent.
- **Jobs-to-be-Done / Ulwick ODI:** customers "hire" products to make progress on a job. Universal 8-step Job Map (Define → Locate → Prepare → Confirm → Execute → Monitor → Modify → Conclude). Desired Outcome statement: `[direction] + [metric] + [context]`. **Opportunity Score = Importance + (Importance − Satisfaction); scores > 10 are underserved.**
- **Obviously Awesome (Dunford):** five components in order — (1) Competitive Alternatives, (2) Unique Attributes, (3) Value (themes, not features), (4) Best-fit Customer Segments, (5) Market Category. Three category options: existing / subcategory / new.
- **Platform Strategy (Parker, Van Alstyne, Choudary):** sides, Core Interaction (Participant + Value Unit + Filter), direct vs cross-side network effects, chicken-and-egg (subsidize harder side), ecosystem flywheel, governance (too loose collapses quality; too strict chokes participation). "Come for the tool, stay for the network."
- **Wardley Mapping:** value chain (user need at top, dependencies below) × evolution (Genesis → Custom-Built → Product → Commodity/Utility). Components always move left to right. Gameplay patterns: ILC (Innovate-Leverage-Commoditize), Ecosystem, Tower and Moat, Signal Distortion.
- **RICE / ICE / Weighted Scoring:** `RICE = (Reach × Impact × Confidence) / Effort`. ICE = Impact × Confidence × Ease. Weighted = Σ(weight × score). Score is an input to decisions, not the decision.
- **Opportunity Solution Trees (Torres):** Outcome → Opportunities (customer needs) → Solutions → Experiments. Weekly customer contact. Test riskiest assumption first across desirability / viability / feasibility / usability.
- **North Star Metric:** one metric that (a) reflects customer value, (b) leads revenue, (c) team can move. Decomposed into 3–5 input metrics by breadth / depth / frequency / efficiency.
- **Kano Model:** Must-be / One-dimensional (Performance) / Attractive (Delighters) / Indifferent / Reverse. Categories drift right over time — today's delighter becomes tomorrow's table stake. Healthy portfolio mixes all three productive types.
- **PLG vs SLG:** spectrum, not binary. Strong PLG signals: ACV < $25K, time-to-value in minutes, buyer = user, low data sensitivity. Strong SLG signals: ACV > $100K, buying committee, high complexity, regulated data. Most enterprise B2B is hybrid.
- **Land-and-Expand:** three expansion vectors — more users, more use cases, more BUs. Land must sit below the "CFO line." Track **Net Dollar Retention (NDR); best-in-class B2B is 130–150%.**
- **Category Entry vs Subcategory vs Category Creation:** category creators that succeed capture ~76% of category market cap (Play Bigger), but most attempts fail. Subcategory ("Universal Semantic Layer") splits the difference — leverage existing awareness, differentiate on a modifier.

## Heuristics

- **Pick by altitude.** Corporate/product strategy → Playing to Win. Customer truth → JTBD. Market frame → Obviously Awesome / Category. Business model → Platform. Landscape → Wardley. Planning → OST / RICE / Kano / North Star. GTM → PLG-vs-SLG / Land-and-Expand.
- **Default sequence for a semantic-layer-style PM:** Playing to Win → JTBD → Obviously Awesome → Wardley → OST → RICE + Kano → North Star → GTM frameworks. Each output feeds the next.
- **Start Obviously Awesome with Competitive Alternatives, not features.** Include "do nothing," "build in-house," "use Excel" — not just direct competitors. Without recent win/loss data it's garbage in, garbage out.
- **JTBD Opportunity Score: Importance + (Importance − Satisfaction).** Top-right quadrant (high importance, low satisfaction) is the real opportunity. Segment by outcome patterns, not demographics.
- **Wardley: invest where things are evolving, not where they sit today.** Genesis layer = competitive advantage; Commodity layer = use, don't build. Watch for Signal Distortion in marketing ("ontology", "semantic layer") that misrepresents evolution stage.
- **RICE's Confidence factor is the bias-killer.** Penalize speculative reach/impact rather than letting a HiPPO frame it as certain. Beware false precision — score is a discussion input, not the verdict.
- **Outcome → Opportunity → Solution → Experiment, in that order.** Most teams jump outcome → solution. Force at least three solutions per top opportunity to defeat anchoring.
- **One North Star, supported by 3–5 input metrics.** Reject revenue (it's a company outcome). Pick the metric harder to game between two equals. Enterprise B2B is harder: low N, long cycles, multi-stakeholder.
- **Kano drifts right.** Yesterday's delighter (autonomous aggregates 2020) is today's performance feature and tomorrow's must-be. Re-classify annually.
- **GTM verdict comes from honest factor assessment, not aspiration.** High ACV + buying committee + heavy procurement = SLG core, with a possible PLG wedge at the developer/agent layer feeding the enterprise pipeline ("open-source bottom, enterprise top" — Snowflake, Databricks, Cube).
- **Land must be below the CFO line and naturally expandable.** "Land and strand" (flat NDR) is the failure mode. NDR > 120% means expansion exceeds churn.
- **Default to subcategory.** Full category creation costs 2–5 years and a lot of money; most attempts fail. A subcategory ("Universal Semantic Layer") leverages existing buyer awareness while owning a differentiator.
- **Coherence test, always.** If Where-to-Play and Capabilities contradict, the strategy isn't a strategy. If positioning and GTM disagree about who the customer is, one of them is wrong.

## When to apply

- Annual strategy reset / product launch — start with Playing to Win, end with Category and GTM.
- Backlog debates — RICE/ICE/Weighted to surface scoring assumptions; Kano to balance must-be vs delighter investment.
- Discovery cadence — weekly OST update; monthly JTBD interview synthesis; quarterly Opportunity Score refresh.
- Build vs buy decisions — Wardley Map; build at Genesis/Custom, buy/rent at Product/Commodity.
- Positioning feels off / win rates dropping — rerun Obviously Awesome from Competitive Alternatives.
- Ecosystem / API / agent strategy questions — Platform Strategy to map sides, Core Interaction, flywheel constraint.
- GTM design at launch or pivot — PLG-vs-SLG factor scoring; design hybrid handoff if applicable.
- Account growth flat — Land-and-Expand playbook by vector; instrument expansion triggers; check NDR.
- Buyers compare you to wrong things — Category decision (Entry / Subcategory / Creation).
- Org alignment broken — set or revisit the North Star Metric and its inputs.

## Out of scope

- Pure financial planning, unit economics, pricing-model math — these frameworks inform but don't compute pricing.
- OKR / MBO mechanics, 1:1s, performance reviews, hiring loops — use high-output-management.
- Strategy critique, kernel construction, diagnosis-guiding-policy-coherent-action — use good-strategy-bad-strategy.
- Pure execution / delivery management, sprint mechanics, project portfolio management — use user-story-mapping for story-level work.
- Detailed competitive teardowns of specific platforms — use databricks-genie-snowflake-cortex-teardown, dbt-metricflow-cube-technical-architecture, or atscale-technical-reference.
