# Your Next Five Moves — Patrick Bet-David

## Core frame

The discipline that separates great founders from average ones is consciously thinking three-to-four moves ahead — a chess-grand-master habit applied to business. The framework has five sequential masteries: (1) know yourself / your future truth; (2) master the ability to reason (Solve-for-X, ITR formula); (3) build the right team (principles-based culture, four levels of trust); (4) strategy to scale (Moneyball measurement, capital-raise filter); (5) master power plays (asymmetric competition, meeting prep, four drivers). The signature insight: **the right move played in the wrong sequence is the wrong move.** Move 15 is rarely correctly played as move 3.

## Key concepts

- **The five moves** — sequential masteries: know yourself → reason → team → scale → power plays.
- **Future truth** — the version of yourself you commit to becoming. The personal-identity audit (25 questions) is the on-ramp.
- **Four phases of company life** — formulation, survival, momentum, plateau. The right move depends on which phase you're in.
- **Solve-for-X** — Bet-David's problem-processing grid: Investigate (root-cause it) → Solve (option-generate) → Implement (commit and act). A 3×3 disciplined alternative to gut decision-making.
- **ITR formula (Investment to Return)** — sized-opportunity rule of thumb: weigh time + money + opportunity cost vs realistic upside. Use before saying yes to anything.
- **Principles-based culture** — a written code of conduct that survives the founder. The test: does it tell people what to do when you're not in the room?
- **Four levels of trust** — Stranger, Endorsed, Trusted, Running Mate. Calibrate what information you share, what authority you grant, by level.
- **Moneyball mindset** — measure inputs your competitors don't measure; arbitrage the gap between what's tracked and what matters.
- **Ten questions before raising capital** — Bet-David's pre-raise filter (do you actually need it? what's the dilution implication? what's the cap-table 3 rounds out? etc.). If you can't answer, don't raise yet.
- **Twelve ways to beat Goliath** — asymmetric-competition playbook for the smaller player; pick the dimension where the incumbent can't or won't compete.
- **Seven-step meeting prep** — mobster-style discipline for high-stakes meetings: research the room, set the goal, anticipate objections, prep the ask, rehearse opening, plan exits, control follow-up.
- **Four drivers of people** — Advancement, Individuality, Madness, Purpose. Lead each team member by which one moves them.
- **Five Deadly Sins of Entrepreneurs** — common founder failure modes (e.g., ego, isolation, refusing to take advice).
- **Four Hazardous G's** — Bet-David's named risk factors (girls/guys, gambling, etc. — translated as the personal-life confounders that tank business judgement).
- **Single-customer concentration rule** — when one customer is >30% of revenue, you're in danger; >60% means it's existential.
- **Fixing-people fallacy** — the belief you can change a wrong hire's fundamental wiring. You can't; replace them.

## Heuristics

- **Always check sequencing first.** Before recommending a move, ask: what phase is the founder in (formulation / survival / momentum / plateau)? The same move can be right in one phase and disastrous in another.
- **Run Solve-for-X on any non-trivial decision.** Investigate the root, generate 3+ options, then commit and implement. Skipping Investigate is the failure mode.
- **Run ITR before saying yes.** Especially to opportunities, partnerships, hires. If the time+money+opportunity-cost calculation doesn't beat your current baseline, decline.
- **Calibrate information by trust level.** Strangers get the public story; Running Mates get the cap table. Mismatch (sharing too much too soon) is a control problem.
- **The 30% rule.** Customer concentration above 30% — start diversifying. Above 60% — that's the only thing that matters.
- **Beat Goliath asymmetrically, not head-on.** Pick a dimension (speed, focus, niche, channel, model) where the incumbent's size is a disadvantage, not an advantage.
- **Don't fix people. Replace people.** If a hire is wrong on values or wiring, the cost of patience usually exceeds the cost of replacement plus rehire.
- **Set principles before scale.** A principles-based culture written down before momentum hits is cheap; trying to retrofit one after 200 hires is impossible.

## When to apply

- **"I'm starting a company / what do I focus on first?"** → Move 1: future truth + personal identity audit. The other moves depend on knowing what you're actually optimizing for.
- **"Should I raise money?"** → Run the ten-question pre-raise filter. If you can't answer 8 of them, you're not ready; the round will hurt you. If you can, decide on size and terms with the cap-table-3-rounds-out test.
- **"How do I beat [bigger competitor]?"** → Twelve ways to beat Goliath. Pick the dimension where the incumbent can't or won't compete. Don't fight on their strength.
- **"We have one customer at 60% of revenue."** → Existential. Move 4: diversify aggressively. Treat customer-concentration as the #1 risk; everything else is secondary until it's <30%.
- **"How do I think about this decision?"** → Solve-for-X. Don't skip Investigate. Generate at least 3 options before committing.
- **"Should I take this opportunity / partner with X?"** → Run ITR. Time + money + opportunity cost vs realistic upside. If the math is fuzzy, decline.
- **"How do I prep for [investor / partner / customer] meeting?"** → Seven-step meeting prep. Research the room, set the goal, anticipate objections, prep the ask. Don't wing it.
- **"My team isn't motivated."** → Four drivers diagnostic per person: Advancement, Individuality, Madness, Purpose. Lead each by their driver, not your default.
- **"Hire vs fire vs coach this person?"** → Apply the fixing-people fallacy test. If it's a values/wiring issue, replace. If it's a TRM issue, coach (and consult high-output-management for the coaching playbook).

## Out of scope

- **Managing an existing large org** — Bet-David's frame is entrepreneurial/founder strategy. For line-management of an existing team, use the high-output-management skill.
- **Corporate strategy critique** — for evaluating an existing strategy document, use the good-strategy-bad-strategy skill (Rumelt's kernel is the sharper tool there).
- **Legal/tax/securities specifics** — references capital raises but doesn't develop the regulatory dimension. Don't extrapolate.
- **Personal life advice** — Bet-David's frame is business decision-making; the identity audit is scoped to business identity, not life coaching.
